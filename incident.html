Incident report: 25 August 2026

Between 03:31 and 08:57 UTC, roughly 115.8M RIO moved out of Realio-controlled wallets on Algorand and Stellar into two accounts created the same morning. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

Situation is live and moving

All figures on this page are a point-in-time reading as at 25 August 2026, 11:05 UTC. Balances were still changing when it was written. The Realio native chain stopped producing blocks at 10:38:05 UTC at height 19,573,265, verified independently against three separate public endpoints, and the team has indicated it is investigating. We have not verified the reason for the halt and are not attributing one.

What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this is consistent with compromise of signing keys held by the platform rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. It is not proof of one, and other explanations may exist. Only Realio can confirm what happened.

Movement out

  • Sold on the Stellar DEX. 491,636.87 RIO sold for 17,207.78 XLM between 08:08:42 and 09:47:14, at roughly 0.035 XLM per RIO. Small against the holdings, but it was still running when this was written.
  • Routed off Algorand. 2,066,161 RIO has left the Algorand account. 1,510,000 of it went through OTJX45HK…MDQ724, an account dormant since January 2025 that forwards its balance on within minutes, into an address holding 14.15M ALGO that receives continuous small deposits from thousands of unrelated addresses. That deposit pattern is characteristic of a centralised exchange hot wallet. We have not identified which exchange and are not naming one.
  • Still in place. 113.0M RIO of the 115.8M has not moved.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

What this does to the supply figure

This is the part that matters for the rest of this site, and it is the part most likely to be misread.

No RIO was created

The Algorand asset is fixed at 100,000,000 and has been immutable since July 2020. Stellar's RIO total is unchanged. Nothing was minted on either chain. Any chart showing a supply increase on 25 August is showing an accounting artefact, not new tokens.

Why a naive count spikes

Circulating supply here is total supply minus balances held in Realio-controlled reserve, treasury and bridge wallets. Empty those wallets and the subtraction shrinks, so the same tokens are silently reclassified as public float. That is arithmetic, not an event.

How we now count it

The 113.0M sitting in the two receiving accounts is held in a separate compromised bucket. It is not counted as circulating float, because it is not a holder deciding whether to sell, and it is not counted as Realio-controlled either, because it plainly is not. It is shown on its own line and never folded into either.

What we do count

RIO the receiving accounts have already sold or forwarded on is counted as circulating, because it genuinely has entered the market. That is why the multichain total moves slightly rather than not at all.

What we got wrong, and what we changed

This site published an incorrect figure for about four and a half hours. It is worth being precise about why.

Our daily job ran unattended at 06:33 UTC, an hour after the Algorand reserve was emptied. It read the reserve at near zero, subtracted almost nothing, and reported Algorand float rising from 7.84M to 51.68M, taking the headline multichain total from 327.3M to 371.1M. The only warning it raised was an unrelated routine one. A 43.8M reserve going to zero passed silently, because nothing was watching for it.

Three changes are now in the open-source pipeline:

  • A movement assertion on every excluded wallet. Any excluded balance moving more than 2%, above a 25,000 RIO noise floor, is flagged loudly rather than quietly reclassified. This check is what found the Stellar leg: we were looking at Algorand and it surfaced the treasury going to zero on a chain we had not thought to check.
  • A compromised bucket, so balances outside Realio's control but not in public hands are reported separately instead of being absorbed into float.
  • Chain liveness on the native reading. A halted chain keeps serving its last committed state, so every figure still returns a number and nothing looks wrong. Native readings now carry block height, block time and age, and a halt is flagged.

The corrected history, the code and the daily snapshots are all in the public repository, including the incorrect reading and its replacement. We have not rewritten it away.

What we cannot say

Whether user funds are lost
We can see balances leave addresses. We cannot see what obligations Realio holds towards the people those balances belonged to, what insurance or reserves exist, or what any recovery might look like. Nothing here should be read as a statement about whether individual users will be made whole.
What the roughly 100M rUSD and 100bn realio.fund USD are worth
Both are assets issued by realio.fund. We do not know what backs either of them, whether they are redeemable, or against what. The USD asset on Stellar shows 14,667 authorised accounts. Until the backing is public, we will not put a dollar figure on either, and neither should anyone else.
Why the native chain halted
We verified that it stopped producing blocks at 10:38:05 UTC and that the team has indicated it is investigating. We have not verified a cause, and a halt during an incident is not by itself evidence of one. It could equally be a deliberate protective measure.
Who is responsible
We do not know and we are not speculating. We publish addresses and transactions because they are public facts. Attribution is a matter for Realio and, if they choose, law enforcement.
Whether other chains are affected
Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Solana and the native chain showed no comparable movement in our 11:03 UTC reading. That is one reading of contract totals and known wallets, not an exhaustive audit, and the native chain is halted so its state is frozen at 10:38.
