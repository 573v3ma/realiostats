Situation update · 11 September 2026 · the network is producing blocks again after the theft

The native chain is producing blocks again, after a sixteen-day halt following the 25 August theft. Realio's CTO Eduardo says this is phase one of the restart only: the chain is back, with no new contract and no change yet to how the incident's funds are held; wallets hacked through the old web app that are delegating cannot undelegate for now, a deliberate protection, and a future migration will move them to new wallets so holders will not need to act themselves. Derek Boirun says there is no recovery process in place or announced yet; a claims dashboard is planned on Freehold but is not live. Incident report: 25 August 2026

Since 03:31 UTC on 25 August, roughly 129.5M RIO has moved out of Realio-controlled wallets and holder accounts across five chains, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Algorand, Stellar and the Realio native chain, into accounts created that morning. It came in three waves, the last of them on 27 August, which also took the native BNB from 709 wallets. Nothing has been swept anywhere since 12:57 on 27 August, and Realio has said the theft is over. The attacker spent the following night selling: he then emptied every liquid balance on 28 August and now holds 46.1M RIO, on Algorand and the native chain only, having extracted about $338,700 in total. The fastest cash-out route was closed within hours, after community members raised the alarm and MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on Algorand, and the native chain was halted 23 minutes after its sweep stopped, staying down for sixteen days until it resumed on 10 September. Neither of the attacker's two remaining balances has moved since. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

This table says where tokens sit, not who is owed what. Those are different questions and it is worth keeping them apart. Two things follow that are easy to miss. First, the 5.73M native portion may not be a permanent loss at all: a Cosmos network that restarts from a pre-incident height can reverse transfers outright, a well-established recovery route. That is not what happened on 10 September: the chain resumed forward from the halt height, so the compromised balance is carried over intact rather than reversed. Whether a future update does something to it is squarely the team's decision, and Eduardo has said more upgrades are coming in phases. Second, if Realio compensates holders from treasury, that would be previously excluded RIO moving into public hands, a new event rather than an unwinding of this one, so it would not change the arithmetic above. We measure what the chain shows today and will update the moment it shows something else.

The MEXC reissuance, and a correction to our own figures

On 11 September Realio minted 50,292,276 RIO on Ethereum. Working out what it was for showed that this site had been subtracting about 48M RIO of ordinary exchange float from its headline for ten weeks.

The mint went to a single address and has not moved. There was no matching burn anywhere: the native chain gained only its usual emission, BNB Chain was unchanged, the deprecated pre-2024 Ethereum contract was unchanged, the Base bridge lock was unchanged, and all three watched Algorand wallets were unchanged. So on its face it was 50.3M RIO of new supply, which is why we went looking.

Realio confirmed the mint is MEXC, preparing to move funds off Algorand because that chain is being retired. The arithmetic backs that up exactly. MEXC holds RIO on Algorand in two wallets:

The first of those is the wallet we had labelled a bridge wallet. It is not a bridge. It is MEXC custody, and three things say so independently of Realio's confirmation. It was funded with 39,843,749.81 RIO in a single transfer straight from the MEXC hot wallet on 24 June 2026, the largest transfer it has ever received. It behaves like an exchange, with 151 distinct depositors and 78 distinct withdrawal destinations across its last 1,000 RIO transactions, and it holds 5.3M ALGO. And its balance, added to the MEXC hot wallet, lands on the mint to within half a token.

This site counts exchange-held customer RIO as circulating, because it is in public hands and can be sold. So that balance should never have been subtracted. It was, from the 5 July revision onward, which means the headline we published between 5 July and 11 September was about 48M too low. The site's own code carried a note that the wallet's classification was unconfirmed and that the figure would be wrong if it turned out to be something else. It was something else.

What we have changed. Both MEXC wallets are counted as float for every date up to the reissuance, and the whole daily history has been restated on that basis. From the reissuance onward the Ethereum tokens are the live ones, and the Algorand units behind them are treated as retired, exactly as this site already treats the deprecated pre-2024 Ethereum contract. That keeps the total continuous across the event: our last reading before the mint was 375,751,508 and the first after it was 375,764,391, a difference of about 12,900 RIO, which is one day of emission. A one-for-one replacement should move the headline by nothing, and it does not.

One consequence of the restatement is worth naming, because it removes a signal that was never real. Our chart used to show Algorand float falling about 40M between March and June 2026. Nothing of the sort happened. That was MEXC moving customer balances from one of its own wallets to another, and we were subtracting the destination.

Two things we would still like answered. The Algorand asset is fixed at 100M and immutable, so the reissued units were not burned and cannot be: they still exist and MEXC can still move them. Nothing double-counts today, because Realio has called RIO on Algorand a dead asset and we treat those units as retired, but that rests on a declaration rather than on anything enforced on-chain. And the Ethereum daily mint cap, raised from 5,000,000 to 55,000,000 to fit this mint into one transaction, has not been lowered back. As of this writing it still reads 55,000,000, against 5,000,000 on BNB Chain.

What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this points to compromise of signing keys held by the platform, rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. Realio has since indicated that realio.fund wallets were drained, which is consistent with what the chain data shows. The precise route in has not been made public, and we are not speculating about it.

  • Cashed out from Stellar. Between 18:39 and 18:52 on 26 August, 144,816 XLM, about $26,300, went to a single exchange deposit address in two transfers carrying numeric deposit memos. That is effectively everything the Stellar selling had raised. Selling stopped one minute later.
  • Routed off Algorand. 2,066,161 RIO has left the Algorand account. 1,510,000 of it went through OTJX45HK…MDQ724, an account dormant since January 2025 that forwards its balance on within minutes, into ZEJPIFQF5M…BWQSLQ: a MEXC deposit address, holding 14.15M ALGO and taking continuous small deposits from thousands of unrelated addresses. The largest single hop, 1,000,000 RIO, landed there at 07:44:07.
  • Sold into the BNB Chain pools. The full 2,203,982.24 RIO taken on BNB Chain left the collecting address within 46 minutes of the sweep ending, in 14 transfers between 05:03:14 and 05:46:49, routed through intermediary contracts into the RIO/USDT and RIO/WBNB liquidity pairs. This is the one leg where the entire take was moved on immediately. That first sweep realised roughly $24,000 in BNB and USDT, about $0.011 per RIO, an 80% discount to spot, which is what selling 2.2M RIO into pools that thin actually costs. A second BNB sweep then took a further 2,976,241 RIO ending at 00:08 on 26 August; 2,501,241 of it still sits at the collecting address.
  • Out to an exchange, from BNB Chain. The 6,127.75 USDT did not stay put. At 07:22:19 UTC it reached a large exchange hot wallet, through two hops and with the amount identical at every step: 0xbe827abf…690a404 to 0x86f0175c…24ac8eb to 0x2873ed87…0ff9fc227 to 0x8894e0a0…49be2d4e3. We are not naming the exchange, because we are inferring it from the wallet rather than from a labelled source, but that last address holds 330.5M USDT and 52,298 BNB across 56.1 million transactions, so it is a top-tier venue and anyone can check the label themselves. Beware the decoys: the same address also sent an identical 6,127.7498 of a fake token, total supply 1,000,000,000,000,000,000, to 0x86f06c05…07c8eb, a lookalike of the real recipient. Address poisoning, designed to send a trace down the wrong path.
  • Still held, now treated two different ways in circulating supply. The attacker holds 46.1M RIO: 40,349,824 on Algorand, which he was selling through a DEX from 27 August, and the 5,732,041 native leg. The Algorand balance is treated as a dead asset, its reserve-origin share of 43,850,860 permanently excluded, following Realio's 27 August post. The 1,646,125 that came out of ordinary holder wallets is counted as circulating whatever the attacker does with it, because Realio intends to let those holders claim their tokens back, so the claim is live. The native leg is counted as circulating despite Realio's validator channel reporting the address blacklisted, because Realio controls that chain and has said a recovery is coming for affected holders. Ethereum, BNB Chain and Stellar are at zero, all sold rather than recovered, though a large share of what was sold on Stellar is treated as permanently excluded too, on the same basis.
Both routes were open, Realio now says the addresses are blacklisted

The Algorand route was closed, and for two days that worked. MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on the Algorand network after community members spotted the movement and raised it with the exchanges. Exchange policy is not something on-chain data can show directly, but the effect worked for two days: no RIO left the Algorand receiving account between 08:29:02 UTC on 25 August and 15:19 on 27 August. That ended on 27 August, when he began selling on an Algorand DEX instead, at an 86% discount, taking the balance down toward the 40,349,824 RIO it now shows. A deposit suspension closes the exchange door, not the ledger, which is what made a further step, the blacklisting Realio now reports, the more meaningful one if it holds up. We could not confirm the blacklist claim ourselves; see below.

Stellar has no equivalent switch. An exchange can suspend deposits because a company decides to. The Stellar DEX is part of the ledger itself: there is no deposit step, no listing to pull, and nobody to ask. The account paused and resumed repeatedly: a pause around 09:47, back at 12:00, heaviest through the afternoon, a pause at 18:14:51, selling again overnight, on through 26 August, and then a final trade at 18:53:16 one minute after the XLM proceeds were sent to an exchange. It resumed on the evening of 27 August and dumped the rest: 67,457,132 RIO for 39,600 XLM by 02:26:50 on 28 August, an average of $0.000108 per RIO. The account now holds zero RIO. Across the incident it sold 71,883,151 RIO for 184,824 XLM, about $34,000.

So the honest scoreboard, in two different currencies. In tokens: of the 129.5M taken, roughly 83M has now reached an exchange, a pool or a buyer. It is made up of 71,883,151 sold on the Stellar DEX, 1,444,091 sold on Ethereum, 5,118,982 moved on BNB Chain and about 4.1M routed off Algorand. In money, far less than that sounds. The Stellar sales raised 184,824 XLM in total, about $34,000, of which 144,816 XLM has already been moved to an exchange. Counting every liquid asset the attacker holds across all five chains, plus what has already been moved out, the total comes to about $338,700 against a nominal $5.5M of RIO taken, roughly 6%. The largest single item is 64.87 ETH on Ethereum, which is not RIO proceeds. The reason is liquidity. Every RIO pool on both EVM chains is a concentrated-liquidity pool, and the selling has drained the side that pays out: about $28,500 of WETH left in the Uniswap V3 RIO/WETH pool on Ethereum, $15,700 of WBNB in the PancakeSwap V3 pool on BNB Chain and $2,700 of USDT in the Uniswap V3 pool there. Roughly $46,800 of buyable depth in total, against 46.1M RIO still held. We are not modelling the slippage, because these are not constant-product pools and a formula would give a false precision; the measured outcome is enough. The BNB Chain leg realised about $0.011 per RIO against spot, an 80% discount. The BNB Chain portion was gone before anyone raised the alarm at all, swept and sold between 04:15 and 05:47. This is not a recovery. Whether it is a freeze is what is in question for the native and Algorand balances still held: Realio's validator channel says both addresses have now been blacklisted, and we could not confirm it ourselves as of this writing. Either way we count the native balance as circulating, because Realio controls that chain and has said a recovery is coming for affected holders. The reserve and treasury-origin share of what was taken on Algorand and Stellar, sold or not, is treated as permanently excluded, on Realio's 27 August post calling RIO on those two chains a dead asset. The share that came out of ordinary holder wallets is counted as circulating on every chain, unconditionally, because those holders are to be allowed to claim their tokens back. Neither figure moves with what the attacker does next, which is the point: a published number should not depend on a thief's trading decisions.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

What the attacker actually got

Out of 129.5M RIO taken, worth about $5.5M at the current price, the attacker extracted roughly $338,713. That is 6.2%, or about one dollar in every sixteen. RIO taken is not money made, and on these chains the gap between the two is enormous. As of 28 August this is no longer a list of what sits in the collecting addresses. Those are empty. The Ethereum RIO was sold and its proceeds left the chain without landing in any of them, so some of that money is not in the table. The BNB Chain balance rose by 22 BNB over the same period, which is the obvious candidate, but we have not proved the link and are not going to present a guess as a trace.

Set that against the 129.5M RIO taken, nominally about $5.5M at the $0.0424 price above. What is left is 46.1M RIO, roughly $2.0M at quoted prices: 40.3M on Algorand and 5.7M on the native chain, which was halted at the time of writing and, now that it has resumed, remains simply untouched. Ethereum, BNB Chain and Stellar are all at zero. That fall is not a recovery. It is RIO sold into markets that could not absorb it, most of it for a fraction of a cent. There is no venue open to them deep enough to turn much of that into money, and after three days of selling there is markedly less than there was. Every RIO pool we can measure on Ethereum and BNB Chain now holds about $46,800 of buyable assets between them, against roughly 2.1M RIO sitting inside those same pools. The deepest pool anywhere, Uniswap V3 RIO/WETH on Ethereum, is down to 11.37 WETH. Across the whole run the Stellar sales cleared at an 88% discount to spot, and the BNB Chain sales on 26 August at about 65%. One figure is missing and cannot be recovered from chain data: 2,066,161 RIO was deposited to MEXC, and what it sold for, if anything, happened on an exchange's internal books.

The 64.87 ETH is the largest single item and it is not RIO proceeds. That address held zero ETH at 03:30 UTC, sixteen minutes before the sweep began, so all of it arrived with the attack. The 263 Ethereum victims lost 32.63 ETH between them. The remaining 32 ETH we cannot yet source, and we are not going to guess at it.

A small realised total is not the same as getting away with it. All four receiving addresses are published on this page, and everything that moves out of them is written to a public ledger permanently. There is no later point at which this becomes private or reversible. The 46.1M RIO still held sits in two of those addresses and cannot be sold at anything near its quoted price on the venues open to it. Four days of selling on Stellar, 71,883,151 RIO across 1,057 trades, produced 184,824 XLM, about $34,000. That is the whole Stellar position, sold for roughly half a percent of its quoted value. The one route that did reach an exchange ended at a hot wallet run by a venue that performs identity checks, and Realio has said it is in contact with MEXC and KuCoin and intends to work with law enforcement. Whether anything is recovered is not a question chain data can answer. What chain data does guarantee is that the trail is complete and stays that way.

The Ethereum leg is bigger than RIO alone

There are two RIO contracts on Ethereum, and both were swept. Alongside 0x94a8b4ee…09f51aa0, the one this site tracks, there is 0xf21661d0…ae4097: same name, same RIO symbol, 74,488,102 supply. It is the old pre-migration token and the market prices it as such, at about $0.00257, roughly 5% of current RIO. The attacker has taken 976,657 of it from 1,410 accounts, but at that price the whole lot is worth about $2,500. It changes the number of people affected, not the money.

The Ethereum leg is now 1,576 addresses. Across both contracts, 1,576 addresses have lost their tokens, in two waves: 121 on the morning of 25 August, then 1,431 more from 22:39 that evening, ending at 03:12 on 26 August. The 1,163,502 RIO figure counts only the tracked contract, and all of it has since been sold. We do not count the legacy contract as RIO supply, and we checked rather than assumed: 74.4M of it sits with real holders rather than in a burn address, but at 5% of RIO’s price it is a separate deprecated asset, and folding it in would overstate supply by about 74M.
