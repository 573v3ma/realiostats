Incident report: 25 August 2026

Since 03:31 UTC on 25 August, roughly 127.9M RIO has moved out of Realio-controlled wallets and holder accounts across five chains, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Algorand, Stellar and the Realio native chain, into accounts created that morning. This is not finished. A second wave began on the evening of 25 August and went on taking wallets on Ethereum until 03:12 UTC on 26 August. Sweeping stopped there, selling of what was taken ran on until 21:14 that night, and everything has been quiet since. The attacker still holds 118.2M RIO and has paused twice before. The fastest cash-out route was closed within hours, after community members raised the alarm and MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on Algorand, and the native chain was halted 23 minutes after its sweep stopped. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

Situation is unresolved, and this page is still being updated

Everything has stopped, for now. Reading at 06:30 UTC on 27 August: no wallet has been emptied on any chain since 03:12 UTC on 26 August, and no RIO has been sold anywhere since 21:14:31 last night. The last movement of any kind was about nine hours before this reading. The account has paused twice before and resumed both times, so this is a pause on the record, not an ending.

The entire Ethereum position was sold. All 1,163,502 RIO taken from Ethereum holders left the attacker's address through DEX aggregator routes, the last of it at 14:24 on 26 August, and the balance there is zero. The largest single sale we decoded moved 154,030 RIO and cleared at about $0.037, close to spot, because Ethereum held the only genuinely deep RIO pool.

Then the Stellar proceeds were cashed out. Between 18:39 and 18:52 on 26 August the attacker moved 144,816 XLM, about $26,300 and effectively everything the Stellar selling had raised, to a deposit address at an exchange, in two transfers carrying numeric deposit memos. Stellar selling stopped one minute later, at 18:53:16, after 4,426,018 RIO across 786 trades for 145,224 XLM. The account still holds 67,784,800 RIO and 958 XLM. We are not naming the venue here while it has had no chance to act on it.

BNB Chain ran a few hours longer and also stopped. A further 1,230,000 RIO was sold through the evening, the last at 21:14:31, leaving 1,271,241 RIO. Nothing has left the Algorand account since 09:17 on 26 August; that pile is unchanged at 43,369,824 RIO.

RIO has recovered. The price is back to $0.0487, from $0.0400 at yesterday's reading and $0.0465 the morning before. That lifts the nominal value of what was taken, and it lifts the value of what the attacker still cannot sell.

Everything taken came from wallets held inside realio.fund. The platform interface has been deactivated for users, so this is not something an individual holder can act on.

What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this points to compromise of signing keys held by the platform, rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. Realio has since indicated that realio.fund wallets were drained, which is consistent with what the chain data shows. The precise route in has not been made public, and we are not speculating about it.

One route was closed. The other is still open

The Algorand route was closed, and that part worked. MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on the Algorand network after community members spotted the movement and raised it with the exchanges. Exchange policy is not something on-chain data can show directly, but the effect is: no meaningful RIO has left the Algorand receiving account since 08:29:02 UTC, and the balance there stands at 43,369,824 RIO.

Stellar has no equivalent switch. An exchange can suspend deposits because a company decides to. The Stellar DEX is part of the ledger itself: there is no deposit step, no listing to pull, and nobody to ask. The account paused and resumed repeatedly: a pause around 09:47, back at 12:00, heaviest through the afternoon, a pause at 18:14:51, selling again overnight, on through 26 August, and then a final trade at 18:53:16 one minute after the XLM proceeds were sent to an exchange. It has been quiet since, and still holds 67,784,800 RIO.

So the honest scoreboard, in two different currencies. In tokens: of the 127.9M taken, roughly 12M has reached an exchange, a pool or a buyer. It is made up of 4,426,018 sold on the Stellar DEX, 1,163,502 sold on Ethereum, about 4.4M moved on BNB Chain and 2,066,161 routed off Algorand to an exchange. In money, far less than that sounds. The Stellar sales raised 145,224 XLM, about $26,400, effectively all of which has now been moved to an exchange. Counting every liquid asset the attacker holds across all five chains, plus what has already been moved out, the total comes to about $316,700 against a nominal $6.2M of RIO taken, roughly 5%. The largest single item is 64.87 ETH on Ethereum, which is not RIO proceeds. The reason is liquidity. Every RIO pool on both EVM chains is a concentrated-liquidity pool, and the selling has drained the side that pays out: about $27,900 of WETH left in the Uniswap V3 RIO/WETH pool on Ethereum, $15,700 of WBNB in the PancakeSwap V3 pool on BNB Chain and $2,200 of USDT in the Uniswap V3 pool there. Roughly $45,800 of buyable depth in total, against 118.2M RIO still held. We are not modelling the slippage, because these are not constant-product pools and a formula would give a false precision; the measured outcome is enough. The BNB Chain leg realised about $0.011 per RIO against spot, an 80% discount. The BNB Chain portion was gone before anyone raised the alarm at all, swept and sold between 04:15 and 05:47. This is not a recovery and it is not a freeze. The only portion genuinely immobilised is the 5,732,041 on the native chain, and only while that chain stays halted.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

A small realised total is not the same as getting away with it. All four receiving addresses are published on this page, and everything that moves out of them is written to a public ledger permanently. There is no later point at which this becomes private or reversible. The 118.2M RIO still held sits in four of those addresses and cannot be sold at anything near its quoted price on the venues open to it. Three days of selling on Stellar, 4,426,018 RIO across 786 trades, produced 145,224 XLM, about $26,400. The one route that did reach an exchange ended at a hot wallet run by a venue that performs identity checks, and Realio has said it is in contact with MEXC and KuCoin and intends to work with law enforcement. Whether anything is recovered is not a question chain data can answer. What chain data does guarantee is that the trail is complete and stays that way.

The Ethereum leg is bigger than RIO alone

There are two RIO contracts on Ethereum, and both were swept. Alongside 0x94a8b4ee…09f51aa0, the one this site tracks, there is 0xf21661d0…ae4097: same name, same RIO symbol, 74,488,102 supply. It is the old pre-migration token and the market prices it as such, at about $0.00257, roughly 5% of current RIO. The attacker has taken 976,657 of it from 1,410 accounts, but at that price the whole lot is worth about $2,500. It changes the number of people affected, not the money.

The Ethereum leg is now 1,576 addresses. Across both contracts, 1,576 addresses have lost their tokens, in two waves: 121 on the morning of 25 August, then 1,431 more from 22:39 that evening, ending at 03:12 on 26 August. The 1,163,502 RIO figure counts only the tracked contract, and all of it has since been sold. We do not count the legacy contract as RIO supply, and we checked rather than assumed: 74.4M of it sits with real holders rather than in a burn address, but at 5% of RIO’s price it is a separate deprecated asset, and folding it in would overstate supply by about 74M.

The BNB Chain leg is reproducible with one query. Take every Transfer log emitted by the RIO contract 0x94a8b4ee5cd64c79d0ee816f467ea73009f51aa0 between blocks 117,900,109 and 118,012,072, group them by recipient, and count how many distinct senders each recipient received from. One address stands out with 163 senders and exactly one transfer from each. Most free BNB Chain endpoints will refuse the query, which is why we missed this leg on the first pass; bsc.rpc.blxrbdn.com serves it in 5,000-block chunks. We publish the block range so the count can be reproduced exactly rather than approximately.
