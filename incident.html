Do not buy RIO on Stellar or Algorand

Realio's Derek Boirun, on 25 August:

"Reminder, do not buy RIO on Stellar or Algorand. You will lose your money."
Independent · community-led · not affiliated with the Realio team

Incident report: 25 August 2026

Between 03:31 and 10:15 UTC on a single morning, roughly 124.4M RIO moved out of Realio-controlled wallets and holder accounts across five chains, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Algorand, Stellar and the Realio native chain, into accounts created the same morning. The fastest cash-out route was closed within hours, after community members raised the alarm and MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on Algorand, and the native chain was halted 23 minutes after its sweep stopped. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

Situation is unresolved, and this page is still being updated

Selling has paused for the second time today. As at 19:15 UTC the last Stellar DEX trade was at 18:14:51, an hour earlier. Across the day the account sold 2,976,145 RIO over 533 trades for 115,296 XLM, and it still holds 68,220,776 RIO. It realised very little for it: about $21,900, an effective $0.0074 per RIO against a spot price of $0.0508, because the Stellar order book is thin enough that continuous selling walks the price down faster than it fills. The pace faded through the evening rather than stopping abruptly: 475,839 RIO in the 15:00 hour, 260,878 in the 16:00, 132,910 in the 17:00, 5,958 in the 18:00. The Algorand side is genuinely static, unchanged at 43,409,824 RIO since 08:29. The distinction still matters: an exchange can suspend deposits, and MEXC and KuCoin did. A native DEX has no such switch.

Other figures on this page are a point-in-time reading as at 25 August 2026, 13:06 UTC, the last complete snapshot. The native chain stopped producing blocks at 10:38:05 UTC at height 19,573,265, 23 minutes after that sweep's last transfer, and we confirmed at 19:44 UTC that it is still stopped at that same height, over nine hours later. Realio's lead developer requested the halt, and has since told validators the chain will stay down possibly for several days while the team investigates, makes sure no further damage can be done, and works out the path forward with the validator set. The 5,732,041 swept on native is frozen for as long as that lasts.

The 10.70M taken from users is 3.27% of the 327.3M circulating supply recorded the day before. Measured against all RIO in existence, roughly 489.3M across every chain, the full 124.4M is about 25%.

This table says where tokens sit, not who is owed what. Those are different questions and it is worth keeping them apart. Two things follow that are easy to miss. First, the 5.73M native portion may not be a permanent loss at all: it is frozen on a halted chain, and a Cosmos network that restarts from a pre-incident height would reverse those transfers outright, which is a well-established recovery route and squarely the team's decision. Second, if Realio compensates holders from treasury, that would be previously excluded RIO moving into public hands, a new event rather than an unwinding of this one, so it would not change the arithmetic above. We measure what the chain shows today and will update the moment it shows something else.

Nothing was minted anywhere. The Algorand asset has been fixed at 100,000,000 and immutable since July 2020, Stellar's RIO total is unchanged, and native supply moved only by scheduled emission. Any chart showing a supply increase on 25 August is showing an accounting artefact, not new tokens.

What we have and have not checked. Ethereum, BNB Chain, Algorand, Stellar and the native chain have each been read transaction by transaction, BNB Chain across 111,964 blocks with no gap. Base and Solana have not. We have not found a log endpoint that serves those queries without a paid key, and we will not call a chain clean on a contract total alone, which is the check that missed Ethereum, BNB Chain and native.

Alongside the RIO the receiving accounts also took 137,340 ALGO, 72,565 XLM, 1,813 USDC, and the rUSD and realioUSD balances. Those last two were essentially the entire unissued supply of each asset, sitting in treasury rather than in user hands, and neither trades on any market, so neither is a realised loss.

What was taken sat inside realio.fund. Check your balance anyway.

On all five chains the dividing line is realio.fund. What was emptied was the reserve and treasury it controlled, plus the wallets its users held RIO in. RIO sitting in a wallet with no realio.fund connection does not appear in any of the five sweeps we traced.

On Ethereum 121 accounts lost their RIO and their ETH between 03:46 and 04:31 UTC. On BNB Chain 163 lost their RIO between 04:15 and 05:01. On the native chain 2,374 were swept between 09:29 and 10:15. Checking costs nothing.

What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this points to compromise of signing keys held by the platform, rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. Realio has since indicated that realio.fund wallets were drained, which is consistent with what the chain data shows. The precise route in has not been made public, and we are not speculating about it.

One route was closed. The other is still open

The Algorand route was closed, and that part worked. MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on the Algorand network after community members spotted the movement and raised it with the exchanges. Exchange policy is not something on-chain data can show directly, but the effect is: no RIO has left the Algorand receiving account since 08:29:02 UTC, and the balance there is unchanged at 43,409,824 RIO.

Stellar has no equivalent switch. An exchange can suspend deposits because a company decides to. The Stellar DEX is part of the ledger itself: there is no deposit step, no listing to pull, and nobody to ask. The account paused selling around 09:47, resumed at 12:00, ran hardest through the afternoon and paused again at 18:14:51. It still holds 68,220,776 RIO, and nothing prevents it starting again.

So the honest scoreboard, in two different currencies. In tokens: of the 124.4M taken, roughly 7.9M has reached an exchange, a pool or a buyer. It is made up of 2,976,145 sold on the Stellar DEX, 2,066,161 routed off Algorand to an exchange, 2,203,982 moved on BNB Chain and 638,286 dispersed on Ethereum. In money, far less than that sounds. The Stellar sales raised about $21,900. Counting every liquid asset the attacker holds across all five chains, plus the USDT already sent to an exchange, the total comes to about $234,000 against a nominal $6.3M of RIO taken, under 4%. Most of that is 64.87 ETH on Ethereum rather than anything sold for RIO. The reason is liquidity: the entire RIO/USDT pool on BNB Chain holds about $4,500, and selling 50,000 RIO into it would clear at a 53% discount. There is no venue on any of these chains deep enough to move this much RIO at anything near its quoted price. The BNB Chain portion was gone before anyone raised the alarm at all, swept and sold between 04:15 and 05:47. This is not a recovery and it is not a freeze. The only portion genuinely immobilised is the 5,732,041 on the native chain, and only while that chain stays halted.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

What the attacker has actually realised

Out of 124.4M RIO taken, worth about $6.3M at today's price, the attacker has realised $234,480. That is 3.7%, or roughly one dollar in every twenty-six. RIO taken is not money made, and on these chains the gap between the two is enormous. What follows is everything they actually got: the liquid assets still sitting in their addresses, plus the USDT already moved out to an exchange, priced at market. The Ethereum leg is bigger than RIO alone

There are two RIO contracts on Ethereum, and both were swept. Alongside 0x94a8b4ee…09f51aa0, the one this site tracks, there is 0xf21661d0…ae4097: same name, same RIO symbol, 74,488,102 supply. It is the old pre-migration token and the market prices it as such, at about $0.00257, roughly 5% of current RIO. The attacker took 472,015 of it from 184 accounts, more accounts than the 121 on the live contract, but worth only about $1,200. It changes the number of people affected, not the money.

So the Ethereum leg is 263 addresses, not 121. Across both contracts and USDC, 263 addresses lost their tokens, 32.63 ETH and 5,757 USDC between them. The 638,286 RIO in the table above counts only the tracked contract. We do not count the legacy contract as RIO supply, and we checked rather than assumed: 74.4M of it sits with real holders rather than in a burn address, but at 5% of RIO’s price it is a separate deprecated asset, and folding it in would overstate supply by about 74M.

The BNB Chain leg is reproducible with one query. Take every Transfer log emitted by the RIO contract 0x94a8b4ee5cd64c79d0ee816f467ea73009f51aa0 between blocks 117,900,109 and 118,012,072, group them by recipient, and count how many distinct senders each recipient received from. Most free BNB Chain endpoints will refuse the query, which is why we missed this leg on the first pass; bsc.rpc.blxrbdn.com serves it in 5,000-block chunks. We publish the block range so the count can be reproduced exactly rather than approximately.
