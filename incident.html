Incident report: 25 August 2026

Since 03:31 UTC on 25 August, roughly 129.5M RIO has moved out of Realio-controlled wallets and holder accounts across five chains, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Algorand, Stellar and the Realio native chain, into accounts created that morning. This is not finished. A second wave began on the evening of 25 August and went on taking wallets on Ethereum until 03:12 UTC on 26 August. Sweeping stopped there and selling ran on until 21:14 that night, then a third wave took 1,620,006 RIO from 379 more accounts on 27 August, followed by a sweep of the native BNB from 709 wallets. The attacker holds 119.6M RIO. The fastest cash-out route was closed within hours, after community members raised the alarm and MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on Algorand, and the native chain was halted 23 minutes after its sweep stopped. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

Situation update · 27 August 2026, 17:05 UTC · selling has restarted, including on Algorand

A third wave ran at midday. After fifteen hours of no movement anywhere, the same address started emptying wallets again today: 5 Ethereum accounts at 12:11:23 for 280,589 RIO, then BNB Chain in two bursts, 178 accounts from 12:19:53 to 12:24:18 and 196 more from 12:37 to 12:42:50, together 1,339,417 RIO. That is 1,620,006 RIO from 379 accounts in half an hour, and it takes the running total to 129.5M. Given the pattern of the last three days, treat these figures as a floor rather than a final count.

Selling restarted this afternoon, and for the first time on Algorand. On BNB Chain, 200,000 RIO went out in two lots of 100,000 at 15:41:56 and 16:16:58, through the same router used yesterday, for 9.98 BNB, about $7,090. That is $0.0355 per RIO against a spot of $0.0482, a 26% discount, and much the best execution of the incident so far. More significant is Algorand: at 15:19:37 and 15:19:59 two lots of 10,000 RIO were sold through Algorand DEX applications for 1,448 ALGO, about $132. That is $0.0066 per RIO, an 86% discount, but it matters out of proportion to the sum. The Algorand pile had not moved since 25 August, and this page has been describing it as static because the exchange route was closed. It is not static any more. Suspending deposits stopped him reaching an exchange; it never stopped him reaching a decentralised one.

Then he came back for the gas. At 12:56:59 to 12:57:33, thirty-four seconds, 709 BNB Chain accounts sent their native BNB to the same address: 17.12 BNB, about $12,150. Median 0.0099 BNB, largest 1.81. This is the one thing the attacker had left alone: on 25 August the BNB was deliberately not taken, and this page recorded that 146 of the first 163 victims still held theirs. He has now gone back for it, and from a far wider set of wallets than today's RIO sweep touched. It is the smallest sum of the whole incident and the clearest sign that the easy money is gone.

The signing access has still not been cut. This is the plainest reading of today's wave. More than two days after the first sweep, and after the platform interface was taken down, the same key is still able to move holder balances on two chains. Everything taken still comes from wallets held inside realio.fund.

The earlier Ethereum position was sold in full. All 1,163,502 RIO taken from Ethereum holders in the first two waves left the attacker's address through DEX aggregator routes, the last of it at 14:24 on 26 August. The balance was zero until today's sweep put 280,589 RIO back into it. The largest single sale we decoded moved 154,030 RIO and cleared at about $0.037, close to spot, because Ethereum held the only genuinely deep RIO pool.

Then the Stellar proceeds were cashed out. Between 18:39 and 18:52 on 26 August the attacker moved 144,816 XLM, about $26,300 and effectively everything the Stellar selling had raised, to a deposit address at an exchange, in two transfers carrying numeric deposit memos. Stellar selling stopped one minute later, at 18:53:16, after 4,426,018 RIO across 786 trades for 145,224 XLM. The account still holds 67,784,800 RIO and 958 XLM. We are not naming the venue here while it has had no chance to act on it.

BNB Chain selling ran a few hours longer, then stopped. A further 1,230,000 RIO was sold through the evening of 26 August, the last at 21:14:31, which cleared at about $0.0148 per RIO against a spot above $0.041, a 65% discount. The balance fell to 1,271,241, today's sweep took it back up to 2,610,658, and this afternoon's selling has brought it to 2,410,658. The Algorand pile is 43,349,824 RIO after this afternoon's first sales there.

RIO is back where it started the day. The price is $0.0482, having dipped to $0.0451 this afternoon, against $0.0400 yesterday and $0.0465 the day before. The dip and the recovery both happened while only 220,000 RIO was being sold, which is a fair measure of how thin this market is in both directions.

This table says where tokens sit, not who is owed what. Those are different questions and it is worth keeping them apart. Two things follow that are easy to miss. First, the 5.73M native portion may not be a permanent loss at all: it is frozen on a halted chain, and a Cosmos network that restarts from a pre-incident height would reverse those transfers outright, which is a well-established recovery route and squarely the team's decision. Second, if Realio compensates holders from treasury, that would be previously excluded RIO moving into public hands, a new event rather than an unwinding of this one, so it would not change the arithmetic above. What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this points to compromise of signing keys held by the platform, rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. Realio has since indicated that realio.fund wallets were drained, which is consistent with what the chain data shows. The precise route in has not been made public, and we are not speculating about it.

Both routes are open again

The Algorand route was closed, and for two days that worked. MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on the Algorand network after community members spotted the movement and raised it with the exchanges. Exchange policy is not something on-chain data can show directly, but the effect worked for two days: no RIO left the Algorand receiving account between 08:29:02 UTC on 25 August and 15:19 on 27 August. That has now ended. He is selling on an Algorand DEX instead, at an 86% discount, and the balance stands at 43,349,824 RIO. A deposit suspension closes the exchange door; it cannot close the ledger.

Stellar has no equivalent switch. An exchange can suspend deposits because a company decides to. The Stellar DEX is part of the ledger itself: there is no deposit step, no listing to pull, and nobody to ask. The account paused and resumed repeatedly: a pause around 09:47, back at 12:00, heaviest through the afternoon, a pause at 18:14:51, selling again overnight, on through 26 August, and then a final trade at 18:53:16 one minute after the XLM proceeds were sent to an exchange. It has been quiet since, and still holds 67,784,800 RIO.

So the honest scoreboard, in two different currencies. In tokens: of the 129.5M taken, roughly 12M has reached an exchange, a pool or a buyer. It is made up of 4,426,018 sold on the Stellar DEX, 1,163,502 sold on Ethereum, about 4.4M moved on BNB Chain and 2,066,161 routed off Algorand to an exchange. In money, far less than that sounds. The Stellar sales raised 145,224 XLM, about $26,400, effectively all of which has now been moved to an exchange. Counting every liquid asset the attacker holds across all five chains, plus what has already been moved out, the total comes to about $342,500 against a nominal $6.2M of RIO taken, roughly 6%. The largest single item is 64.87 ETH on Ethereum, which is not RIO proceeds. The reason is liquidity. Every RIO pool on both EVM chains is a concentrated-liquidity pool, and the selling has drained the side that pays out: about $28,500 of WETH left in the Uniswap V3 RIO/WETH pool on Ethereum, $15,700 of WBNB in the PancakeSwap V3 pool on BNB Chain and $2,700 of USDT in the Uniswap V3 pool there. Roughly $46,800 of buyable depth in total, against 119.6M RIO still held. We are not modelling the slippage, because these are not constant-product pools and a formula would give a false precision; the measured outcome is enough. The BNB Chain leg realised about $0.011 per RIO against spot, an 80% discount. The BNB Chain portion was gone before anyone raised the alarm at all, swept and sold between 04:15 and 05:47. This is not a recovery and it is not a freeze. The only portion genuinely immobilised is the 5,732,041 on the native chain, and only while that chain stays halted.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

Out of 129.5M RIO taken, worth about $6.2M at the current price, the attacker has realised roughly $342,458. That is 5.5%, or about one dollar in every eighteen. RIO taken is not money made, and on these chains the gap between the two is enormous. About $2,400 of the rise is ETH, BNB and XLM being worth more, and roughly $1,600 is USDT and USDC arriving on BNB Chain. The reference point for what today's RIO is likely to fetch is yesterday evening's BNB Chain selling: 1,230,000 RIO for 25.85 BNB, about $18,190, which is $0.0148 per RIO against a spot above $0.041 at the time. A 65% discount.

This total is a floor, not a ceiling. It counts what sits in addresses we have identified. The Ethereum RIO was sold and its proceeds left the chain without landing in any of them, so some of that money is not in the table. The BNB Chain balance rose by 22 BNB over the same period, which is the obvious candidate, but we have not proved the link and are not going to present a guess as a trace.

Set that against the 129.5M RIO taken, nominally about $6.2M at the $0.0482 price above. They still hold roughly $5.8M of RIO at quoted prices: 67.8M on Stellar, 43.3M on Algorand, 2.4M on BNB Chain, 0.3M on Ethereum and 5.7M frozen on the halted native chain. There is no venue open to them deep enough to turn much of that into money, and after three days of selling there is markedly less than there was. Every RIO pool we can measure on Ethereum and BNB Chain now holds about $46,800 of buyable assets between them, against roughly 2.1M RIO sitting inside those same pools. The deepest pool anywhere, Uniswap V3 RIO/WETH on Ethereum, is down to 11.37 WETH. Across the whole run the Stellar sales cleared at an 88% discount to spot, and the BNB Chain sales on 26 August at about 65%. One figure is missing and cannot be recovered from chain data: 2,066,161 RIO was deposited to MEXC, and what it sold for, if anything, happened on an exchange's internal books.

The 64.87 ETH is the largest single item and it is not RIO proceeds. That address held zero ETH at 03:30 UTC, sixteen minutes before the sweep began, so all of it arrived with the attack. The 263 Ethereum victims lost 32.63 ETH between them. The remaining 32 ETH we cannot yet source, and we are not going to guess at it.

A small realised total is not the same as getting away with it. All four receiving addresses are published on this page, and everything that moves out of them is written to a public ledger permanently. There is no later point at which this becomes private or reversible. The 119.6M RIO still held sits in four of those addresses and cannot be sold at anything near its quoted price on the venues open to it. Three days of selling on Stellar, 4,426,018 RIO across 786 trades, produced 145,224 XLM, about $26,400. The one route that did reach an exchange ended at a hot wallet run by a venue that performs identity checks, and Realio has said it is in contact with MEXC and KuCoin and intends to work with law enforcement. Whether anything is recovered is not a question chain data can answer. What chain data does guarantee is that the trail is complete and stays that way.

The Ethereum leg is bigger than RIO alone

There are two RIO contracts on Ethereum, and both were swept. Alongside 0x94a8b4ee…09f51aa0, the one this site tracks, there is 0xf21661d0…ae4097: same name, same RIO symbol, 74,488,102 supply. It is the old pre-migration token and the market prices it as such, at about $0.00257, roughly 5% of current RIO. The attacker has taken 976,657 of it from 1,410 accounts, but at that price the whole lot is worth about $2,500. It changes the number of people affected, not the money.

The Ethereum leg is now 1,576 addresses. Across both contracts, 1,576 addresses have lost their tokens, in two waves: 121 on the morning of 25 August, then 1,431 more from 22:39 that evening, ending at 03:12 on 26 August. The 1,163,502 RIO figure counts only the tracked contract, and all of it has since been sold. We do not count the legacy contract as RIO supply, and we checked rather than assumed: 74.4M of it sits with real holders rather than in a burn address, but at 5% of RIO’s price it is a separate deprecated asset, and folding it in would overstate supply by about 74M.

The BNB Chain leg is reproducible with one query. Take every Transfer log emitted by the RIO contract 0x94a8b4ee5cd64c79d0ee816f467ea73009f51aa0 between blocks 117,900,109 and 118,012,072, group them by recipient, and count how many distinct senders each recipient received from.
