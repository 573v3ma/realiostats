Incident report: 25 August 2026

Between 03:31 and 08:57 UTC, roughly 115.8M RIO moved out of Realio-controlled wallets on Algorand and Stellar into two accounts created the same morning. The fastest cash-out route was closed within hours, after community members raised the alarm and MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on Algorand. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

Situation is live and moving

All figures on this page are a point-in-time reading as at 25 August 2026, 11:50 UTC. No RIO has left either receiving account since 08:29 UTC, and no sale has been recorded since 09:47 UTC. The Realio native chain stopped producing blocks at 10:38:05 UTC at height 19,573,265, verified independently against three separate public endpoints. Realio's lead developer requested the halt as a precautionary measure while the team investigates.

115.8M RIO is roughly 24% of all RIO in existence across every chain, against 489.3M on the previous day's reading. It is not 35% of circulating supply, and an earlier version of this page said so, wrongly. Almost all of what was taken, 113.7M or 98% of it, was sitting in reserve and treasury wallets that this site already excludes from circulating supply, so it was never counted as float in the first place. Only about 2.1M came out of wallets counted as public float, which is 0.65% of the 327.3M circulating. That is why the corrected circulating figure moved by under 1M rather than collapsing. Of the total taken, 113.0M was still sitting in the two receiving accounts at 11:50 UTC, unsold; the remainder has been forwarded on or sold, detailed below. The rUSD and realioUSD figures need care rather than a headline. Both are realio.fund's own USD-pegged units, and in each case what was taken was essentially the entire unissued supply sitting in the treasury, not user balances. Users held about 39,000 units between them across both chains: 32,547 realioUSD on Stellar spread over 14,122 accounts, and 6,592 rUSD on Algorand. Neither asset trades on any market. The material exposure is the RIO. Nothing was minted on either chain: the Algorand asset has been fixed at 100,000,000 and immutable since July 2020, and Stellar's RIO total is unchanged, so any chart showing a supply increase on 25 August is showing an accounting artefact rather than new tokens. Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Solana and the Realio native chain show no comparable movement: their balances sit within normal daily drift.

RIO in your own wallet is not affected

The dividing line here is custody, not chain. What was drained were balances held inside realio.fund: the platform's own reserve and treasury, and thousands of custodial sub-accounts it created for users. RIO you hold in a wallet whose keys you control was not touched. That includes RIO on BNB Chain, Ethereum and the Realio native chain, where contract totals and native supply sit within normal daily drift in our 11:03 UTC reading, and it includes self-custodied RIO on Algorand and Stellar, which was not swept. If your RIO sits inside realio.fund rather than in a wallet you control, treat it as exposed until the team says otherwise. This describes what the chain data shows at 11:03 UTC on 25 August, not a guarantee about what happens next.

What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this points to compromise of signing keys held by the platform, rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. Realio has since indicated that realio.fund wallets were drained, which is consistent with what the chain data shows. The precise route in has not been made public, and we are not speculating about it.

The cash-out route was closed

MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on the Algorand network after community members spotted the movement and raised it with the exchanges. That is reported by the community and by Realio rather than something on-chain data can show directly, but the effect is visible on-chain: no RIO has left the Algorand receiving account since 08:29:02 UTC, and no sale has been recorded on the Stellar DEX since 09:47:14 UTC. As at 11:50 UTC both accounts are static.

Worth being precise about what this does and does not mean. Deposit suspensions on two exchanges and one network close the fastest exit, and on the evidence they closed it early: of 115.8M RIO taken, roughly 2.5M reached an exchange or a buyer. It is not a recovery and it is not a freeze. The attacker still controls 113.0M RIO, other venues and other networks exist, and the Stellar holding is untouched by an Algorand deposit suspension.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

What we cannot say

Whether user funds are lost
We can see balances leave addresses. We cannot see what obligations Realio holds towards the people those balances belonged to, what insurance or reserves exist, or what any recovery might look like. Nothing here should be read as a statement about whether individual users will be made whole.
What the rUSD and realioUSD balances actually are
Not what the headline numbers suggest, and the difference matters. Realio's own published stellar.toml describes realioUSD as a USD-pegged stablecoin, purchasable and redeemable for dollars by KYC-approved accounts at realio.fund. rUSD is the Algorand equivalent. Neither trades on any market. Crucially, the enormous figures are mint ceilings, not dollars: realio.fund issued each asset at a large round supply and held the unissued remainder in treasury, and it is that remainder the attacker took. On Stellar the receiving account holds 99,999,967,453 of the 99,999,999,999 that exist, which is 99.99997% of the asset, leaving 32,547 units spread across 14,122 user accounts at an average of about 2.30 each. On Algorand the same shape: 99,993,408 of 100,000,000 taken, leaving 6,592 units across 126 accounts with a balance. So roughly 39,000 units of user-held balance are involved, not a hundred billion of anything. We cannot verify what currently backs either asset and are not putting a dollar value on them. The same pattern runs through the rest of that issuer's assets, rBTC at 21,000,000 and rALGO at 10,000,000,000 among them, each minted at the underlying's nominal supply and left in treasury; none of those were touched.
Why the native chain halted
We verified independently that it stopped producing blocks at 10:38:05 UTC at height 19,573,265. Realio's lead developer requested the halt as a precautionary measure while the team investigates. That is the team's account of their own decision, relayed to us rather than something on-chain data can confirm, and we report it as such. A deliberate protective halt is a containment step, not evidence of further compromise.
Who is responsible
We do not know and we are not speculating. We publish addresses and transactions because they are public facts. Attribution is a matter for Realio and, if they choose, law enforcement.
Whether other chains are affected
Ethereum, BNB Chain, Base, Solana and the Realio native chain showed no comparable movement in our 11:03 UTC reading, so RIO held on those chains outside realio.fund does not appear to be affected. Two caveats we will not paper over: that is one reading of contract totals and known wallets rather than an exhaustive audit of every holder, and the native chain is halted, so its state is frozen at block 19,573,265 and cannot change while the halt lasts. The exposure that matters is custodial, not chain-specific, so the question to ask is not which chain your RIO is on but who holds the keys.
