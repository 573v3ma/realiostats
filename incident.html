Incident report: 25 August 2026

Since 03:31 UTC on 25 August, roughly 127.9M RIO has moved out of Realio-controlled wallets and holder accounts across five chains, Ethereum, BNB Chain, Algorand, Stellar and the Realio native chain, into accounts created that morning. This is not finished. A second wave began on the evening of 25 August and was still taking wallets on Ethereum at 05:47 UTC on 26 August. The fastest cash-out route was closed within hours, after community members raised the alarm and MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on Algorand, and the native chain was halted 23 minutes after its sweep stopped. This page records what the public chain data shows, with every address and transaction ID, so anyone can check it independently.

Situation is unresolved, and this page is still being updated

A second wave is under way, and it is larger than the first. Reading at 05:50 UTC on 26 August: wallets are still being emptied on Ethereum, most recently at 05:47:59, three minutes before we looked. On BNB Chain a second sweep ran to 00:08:03 and took 2,976,241 RIO from 1,574 accounts, more than the entire first BNB sweep. On Ethereum the sweep resumed at 22:39 on 25 August and has taken 1,431 accounts that were not touched the first time.

Everything taken still comes from wallets held inside realio.fund. The platform interface has been deactivated for users, so this is not something an individual holder can act on. What it does mean is that the signing access was never cut: nearly 24 hours after the first sweep, the same address is still able to move holder balances on two chains.

Selling continues alongside it. The Stellar account has now sold 3,305,366 RIO across 546 trades for 126,819 XLM, most recently at 02:59:08 on 26 August, and still holds 68,955,694 RIO. Overnight the attacker also moved 141,000 ALGO and 2,172 USDC off Algorand. The Algorand RIO pile is close to unchanged at 43,379,824.

Other figures on this page are a point-in-time reading as at 25 August 2026, 13:06 UTC, the last complete snapshot. The native chain stopped producing blocks at 10:38:05 UTC at height 19,573,265, 23 minutes after that sweep's last transfer, and we confirmed at 19:44 UTC that it is still stopped at that same height, over nine hours later. Realio's lead developer requested the halt, and has since told validators the chain will stay down possibly for several days while the team investigates, makes sure no further damage can be done, and works out the path forward with the validator set. The 5,732,041 swept on native is frozen for as long as that lasts.

This table says where tokens sit, not who is owed what. Those are different questions and it is worth keeping them apart. Two things follow that are easy to miss. First, the 5.73M native portion may not be a permanent loss at all: it is frozen on a halted chain, and a Cosmos network that restarts from a pre-incident height would reverse those transfers outright, which is a well-established recovery route and squarely the team's decision. Second, if Realio compensates holders from treasury, that would be previously excluded RIO moving into public hands, a new event rather than an unwinding of this one, so it would not change the arithmetic above. We measure what the chain shows today and will update the moment it shows something else.

On all five chains the dividing line is realio.fund. What was emptied was the reserve and treasury it controlled, plus the wallets its users held RIO in. RIO sitting in a wallet with no realio.fund connection does not appear in any of the five sweeps we traced.

What the pattern indicates

We describe only what the chain data supports. The cause has not been confirmed by anyone, and we are not asserting one.

The swept accounts look platform-generated

Every Algorand source account we sampled holds exactly 0.20 ALGO. That is the protocol minimum for an account (0.1) plus one asset opt-in (0.1). Thousands of accounts sitting at precisely the minimum, each opted into a single Realio asset, is the shape of automatically created custodial sub-accounts, one per user, not of independent self-custody wallets.

No accounts were rekeyed

Algorand lets an account hand signing authority to another key, which is the signature of a phishing or approval-drainer campaign. We checked a sample of swept accounts and found no rekeying: auth-addr was null on every one. Whoever moved the funds could already sign for them.

Both chains, one operation

The Stellar receiving account was created by the treasury at 03:31, the Algorand one at 04:52, and the last sweeps on both chains land 18 minutes apart. Two separate chains with separate key systems were drained inside the same window.

Deposits kept arriving during the drain

Both drained wallets continued receiving inbound transfers after they were emptied, and those later deposits were swept too. Whatever front end feeds those addresses was still accepting funds while the drain was in progress.

Taken together this points to compromise of signing keys held by the platform, rather than an exploit of a smart contract or a campaign against individual users. Realio has since indicated that realio.fund wallets were drained, which is consistent with what the chain data shows. The precise route in has not been made public, and we are not speculating about it.

One route was closed. The other is still open

The Algorand route was closed, and that part worked. MEXC and KuCoin suspended RIO deposits on the Algorand network after community members spotted the movement and raised it with the exchanges. Exchange policy is not something on-chain data can show directly, but the effect is: no meaningful RIO has left the Algorand receiving account since 08:29:02 UTC, and the balance there stands at 43,379,824 RIO.

Stellar has no equivalent switch. An exchange can suspend deposits because a company decides to. The Stellar DEX is part of the ledger itself: there is no deposit step, no listing to pull, and nobody to ask. The account has paused and resumed repeatedly: a pause around 09:47, back at 12:00, heaviest through the afternoon, a pause at 18:14:51, then selling again overnight to 02:59 on 26 August. It still holds 68,955,694 RIO.

So the honest scoreboard, in two different currencies. In tokens: of the 127.9M taken, roughly 9.1M has reached an exchange, a pool or a buyer. It is made up of 3,305,366 sold on the Stellar DEX, 2,678,982 moved on BNB Chain, 2,066,161 routed off Algorand to an exchange and 1,009,472 dispersed on Ethereum. In money, far less than that sounds. The Stellar sales raised about $23,300. Counting every liquid asset the attacker holds across all five chains, plus what has already been moved out, the total comes to about $263,600 against a nominal $5.9M of RIO taken, roughly 4%. The largest single item is 64.88 ETH on Ethereum, which is not RIO proceeds. The reason is liquidity. Every RIO pool on both EVM chains is a concentrated-liquidity pool, and they are small: about $54,700 in the Uniswap V3 RIO/WETH pool on Ethereum, $30,700 in the PancakeSwap V3 RIO/WBNB pool on BNB Chain and $4,000 in a Uniswap V3 RIO/USDT pool there. Roughly $90,000 of on-chain depth in total, against 118.1M RIO still held. We are not modelling the slippage, because these are not constant-product pools and a formula would give a false precision; the measured outcome is enough. The BNB Chain leg realised about $0.011 per RIO against spot, an 80% discount. The BNB Chain portion was gone before anyone raised the alarm at all, swept and sold between 04:15 and 05:47. This is not a recovery and it is not a freeze. The only portion genuinely immobilised is the 5,732,041 on the native chain, and only while that chain stays halted.

Neither the Algorand asset nor the Stellar asset has clawback enabled. On Stellar the issuer carries auth_clawback_enabled: false and auth_immutable: true, so that cannot be changed later. There is no on-chain mechanism by which these transfers can be reversed.

What the attacker actually got

Out of 127.9M RIO taken, worth about $5.9M at the current price, the attacker has realised roughly $263,596. That is 4.4%, or about one dollar in every twenty-three. RIO taken is not money made, and on these chains the gap between the two is enormous. What follows is everything they actually got: the liquid assets still sitting in their addresses, plus the USDT already moved out to an exchange, priced at market. A small realised total is not the same as getting away with it. All four receiving addresses are published on this page, and everything that moves out of them is written to a public ledger permanently. There is no later point at which this becomes private or reversible. The 118.1M RIO still held sits in three of those addresses and cannot be sold at anything near its quoted price on the venues open to it, which is why two days of selling produced about $23,300. The one route that did reach an exchange ended at a hot wallet run by a venue that performs identity checks, and Realio has said it is in contact with MEXC and KuCoin and intends to work with law enforcement. Whether anything is recovered is not a question chain data can answer. What chain data does guarantee is that the trail is complete and stays that way.

The Ethereum leg is bigger than RIO alone

There are two RIO contracts on Ethereum, and both were swept. Alongside 0x94a8b4ee…09f51aa0, the one this site tracks, there is 0xf21661d0…ae4097: same name, same RIO symbol, 74,488,102 supply. It is the old pre-migration token and the market prices it as such, at about $0.00257, roughly 5% of current RIO. The attacker has taken 976,657 of it from 1,410 accounts, but at that price the whole lot is worth about $2,500. It changes the number of people affected, not the money.

The Ethereum leg is now 1,576 addresses. Across both contracts, 1,576 addresses have lost their tokens, in two waves: 121 on the morning of 25 August, then 1,431 more from 22:39 that evening, still running at 05:47 on 26 August. The 1,163,502 RIO in the table above counts only the tracked contract. We do not count the legacy contract as RIO supply, and we checked rather than assumed: 74.4M of it sits with real holders rather than in a burn address, but at 5% of RIO’s price it is a separate deprecated asset, and folding it in would overstate supply by about 74M.
